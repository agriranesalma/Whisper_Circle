Q: Do I need special soap or products to clean my vagina?
A: No. The vagina cleans itself naturally — it's often called a "self-cleaning oven" by doctors because it maintains its own healthy balance of bacteria. You only need to wash the outside (the vulva) with warm water. Scented soaps, sprays, and cleansing products are not necessary and can actually cause irritation or infection.
Source: https://www.mayoclinichealthsystem.org/hometown-health/speaking-of-health/you-dont-need-fancy-products-for-good-feminine-hygiene

Q: Is douching (washing inside the vagina) safe or recommended?
A: No. Doctors advise against douching. The vagina has a natural acidic environment that protects against harmful bacteria, and douching disrupts this balance. This can actually increase the risk of infections like bacterial vaginosis and yeast infections — the exact problems many people douche trying to prevent.
Source: https://www.mayoclinic.org/symptoms/vaginal-discharge/basics/definition/sym-20050825

Q: Is having vaginal discharge normal, or does it mean something is wrong?
A: Vaginal discharge is completely normal and healthy. Most people with vaginas have some discharge every day — typically clear to white, without a strong odor. It contains mucus, fluid, and healthy bacteria that help protect against infection. There is no reason to feel ashamed of it; it's simply your body taking care of itself.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-discharge-when-to-watch-when-to-work-up/

Q: When is vaginal discharge NOT normal, and I should see a doctor?
A: See a healthcare provider if your discharge changes color (gray, green, or yellow), has a strong or fishy odor, is accompanied by itching or burning, or comes with pain during urination. These can be signs of an infection such as bacterial vaginosis, a yeast infection, or a sexually transmitted infection — all of which are common and treatable.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-discharge-when-to-watch-when-to-work-up/

Q: Should I use scented pads, wipes, or perfume near my vagina to smell "fresh"?
A: No. Scented products (soaps, sprays, wipes) can irritate the sensitive skin of the vulva and disrupt the vagina's natural bacterial balance, which can cause more odor and discharge, not less. A healthy vagina has a mild, natural scent — it is not supposed to smell like perfume, and trying to make it do so often backfires.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-discharge-when-to-watch-when-to-work-up/

Q: What is a yeast infection, and is it something to be ashamed of?
A: A yeast infection happens when the natural balance of bacteria and yeast in the vagina is disrupted — often by antibiotics, scented products, or douching — leading to an overgrowth of yeast (Candida). Signs include itching, a thick white discharge, and burning. It is extremely common, not caused by poor hygiene or anything to feel ashamed about, and is easily treated with over-the-counter or prescription medication.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-health-and-hygiene/

---

Q: What is bacterial vaginosis (BV)?
A: Bacterial vaginosis happens when harmful bacteria outnumber the healthy bacteria in the vagina, often triggered by douching or disrupting the natural balance. It can cause itching, discharge, a fishy odor, or a burning feeling when urinating. It is common, not a sign of poor hygiene, and is treated with antibiotics from a doctor.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-health-and-hygiene/

Q: What kind of underwear or clothing is best for vaginal health?
A: Cotton underwear and loose-fitting clothing are best, since they allow air flow and reduce moisture buildup. Tight synthetic fabrics, thongs, or pantyhose without a cotton crotch can trap moisture and irritation, especially for people prone to infections.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-discharge-when-to-watch-when-to-work-up/

Q: Do "vaginal wellness" products like yoni pearls, steams, or detoxes actually work?
A: Most of these products have no real scientific research behind them, according to gynecologists, and some can actually be harmful — for example, inserting herbal "yoni pearls" can lead to irritation or infection instead of the promised benefits. The vagina does not need detoxing; it already regulates itself naturally.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-health-and-hygiene/

Q: Is it normal for my vaginal discharge to change throughout the month?
A: Yes. Discharge naturally increases and changes in texture around ovulation (the middle of your cycle), becoming thicker and more noticeable. This is a normal hormonal pattern, not a sign of a problem, as long as there's no bad odor, itching, or unusual color.
Source: https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/women-health/vaginal-discharge-when-to-watch-when-to-work-up/

Q: Can washing my underwear with strong detergent or fabric softener cause irritation?
A: Yes. Fragrances, dyes, and softeners in laundry products can irritate sensitive vulvar skin. If you experience recurring irritation, try washing underwear separately in a mild, fragrance-free detergent, and avoid bleach and fabric softener on that laundry load.
