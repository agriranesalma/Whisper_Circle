Q: whats actually an sti and are they super rare
A: An STI is an infection passed through sexual contact — things like chlamydia, HPV, herpes, HIV. They're way more common than people think, and having one doesn't say anything bad about a person. A lot of them show zero symptoms, which is exactly why testing matters even when you feel totally fine.
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/sti/about/index.html

Q: whats the actual best way to protect myself from stis
A: Condoms used correctly every time, staying up to date on vaccines like the HPV one, and getting tested regularly especially with a new partner. That combo is genuinely your strongest protection.
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/std/prevention

Q: does the pill protect me from stis too
A: No — the pill (or IUD, implant, etc) only prevents pregnancy, it does nothing against STIs. Condoms are the only method that protects against both, so using them together with another method gives you the strongest combined protection.
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/std/prevention

Q: whats actually the most effective type of birth control
A: Implants and IUDs are the most effective, both under 1% failure rate since there's nothing to remember day to day. The pill is around 91-99% effective depending how consistently it's taken. Condoms are around 82-98%. Pulling out or nothing at all are way less reliable. A doctor or clinic can help you figure out what actually fits your life.
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/reproductivehealth/contraception/

Q: whats the morning after pill and when would someone actually need it
A: It's emergency contraception used after unprotected sex or if something like a condom broke — it lowers pregnancy risk but works best the sooner you take it, usually within 3 days. Often available at pharmacies without needing a prescription depending where you live. Not the same thing as an abortion pill.
Source: https://www.womenshealth.gov/a-z-topics/emergency-contraception
